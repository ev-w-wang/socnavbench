=====================================

use_gpu                         true   (RTX 3080, torch 1.10.2+cu113)

Why these changes
-----------------
num_samples = 4096
  GPU rollouts are batched across samples and sequential across time. On this
  3080, 256 and 4096 samples both planned in ~39 ms at horizon 40. Sixteen
  times more Monte Carlo draws is the cheap GPU-native upgrade and is the
  change most likely to recover the dense ETH / Univ pedestrian misses under
  ORCA prediction.

horizon = 60 (2.0 s -> 3.0 s)
  Ground-truth MPPI already had zero pedestrian collisions at 256 samples.
  The remaining failure, t_univ_trapTL, is a ~180 deg U-turn in a tight
  alcove. At w_max=1.1 rad/s that turn takes ~2.85 s, which a 2.0 s horizon
  cannot see. Horizon is sequential on GPU (~1.5x plan time, still near the
  50 ms tick). This is the smallest look-ahead that covers that maneuver.
  Iterations stayed at 2: extra iterations are also sequential and samples
  already grew 16x.

initial_sequence_angular_fraction = 1.0
  The goal-directed warm start was spinning at 0.55 rad/s (half of w_max),
  so even the nominal opening turn could not finish inside the old horizon.
  Using the full yaw limit only changes the first plan's prior, not the
  dynamics or costs.

Left unchanged on purpose
-------------------------
static clearance / weight: raising them can close Univ/Hotel corridors that
  already succeed. trapTL is first a horizon/kinematics issue.
dynamic clearance / weight: already high from the 10-episode GT tune; more
  samples should find safer ORCA paths without making the robot slower
  everywhere.
temperature: more samples already improve the weighted average; lowering
  temp without a sweep risks peaky updates.
v_min = 0: allowing reverse would help trapTL but is not a marginal MPPI
  change and would break the Dubins comparison with the paper methods.
num_iterations: sequential, not the GPU-cheap axis.

Reading
-------
This is a sidegrade on the oracle, a small upgrade on ORCA, and a GPU
validity check.

The horizon/init-yaw change did what it was for: t_univ_trapTL succeeds
under both predictors (~29 s, path ratio ~1.6 — a slow U-turn, not a
wall graze). t_eth_dense_flow also cleared on ORCA. The new miss,
t_dhotel_bottopmid, is the same pedestrian clip on GT and ORCA (~4.2 s,
~4.4 m, closest ped ~0.36 m). That is a different sampled path, not a
GPU numerics bug: CPU/GPU RNGs differ, and the longer, more aggressive
opening turn changes the early trajectory. Path, time, and energy stay
in the CPU band. Planner wait dropped ~10x (269->28 s GT, 246->22 s
ORCA). Horizon 60 is why wait is ~2x the 256-sample GPU 5-episode
figure, not 16x from sample count.

Taken as a baseline: GPU MPPI matches CPU behavior closely enough to
trust, oracle ceiling is 32/33, deployable ORCA-pred is 30/33, and
further success chasing is sampling/cost tradeoff rather than a
CPU-vs-GPU issue.
